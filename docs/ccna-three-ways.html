The Book

The course as a beautifully made book. You read; diagrams move when you touch them; questions sit inside the text, and the margin remembers when each one comes back.

Volume II Switching, Routing and Wireless

3.4One cable, many VLANs

An access port carries one VLAN. A trunk carries all of them on the same cable, so every frame needs a label saying which VLAN it belongs to.

That label is the 802.1Q tag: four bytes the switch slips into the frame, holding a twelve-bit VLAN number.1 The switch at the far end reads the tag, takes it out again, and delivers the frame only inside that VLAN.

Figure 3.4 A frame from Sales gets its tag where it enters the trunk.

One VLAN is allowed to cross untagged: the native VLAN, VLAN 1 unless you change it. Both ends must agree on which VLAN that is, or frames quietly land in the wrong one.2

The trunk's native VLAN is 1. How does a frame from PC-SALES cross it?

Tagged with VLAN 10. Only the native VLAN crosses untagged, and Sales is in VLAN 10.

Say it to yourself first.

Next you will give each VLAN a door into the router, on one physical port. That is router-on-a-stick, and it is the lab at the end of this chapter.

A session

  • The app opens on the page you stopped at, marked by the ribbon.
  • You read two or three short pages and touch the figures.
  • Questions from earlier chapters wait in the text where you first met them.

Why it feels like learning

It is the feeling of a good textbook at a quiet desk, with the boring parts done for you: diagrams that move, and a margin that tracks your memory.

The risk

Reading is passive. Labs have to be pulled into the book as worked pages, or it turns into a pleasant e-reader.

Why it feels like learning

Writing it yourself is how it sticks. Over months the notebook becomes yours: your words, linked the way you understand them.

The risk

It asks for effort every session. On a tired evening, a blank "in my words" box can feel like homework.
