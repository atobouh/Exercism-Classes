What a day with it feels like

You should never have to decide what to study. The app decides from what you are forgetting and what you are about to unlock, and you just show up.

  1. 07:40
    Open the app.

    Today already holds one composed session sized to your time budget: reviews first, then one drill, then a lab. One button starts it.

  2. 07:41
    Review.

    Spaced-repetition cards, including any level that is fading. Space reveals, 1 to 4 grades. No mouse needed.

  3. 07:53
    Drill.

    Timed generated questions on your slowest skill, so speed improves where it is weakest.

  4. 08:01
    Lab.

    Configure real IOS syntax, then step a packet through the network and predict each hop before it moves.

  5. 08:27
    Recap and close.

    What changed in your knowledge network, one line of reflection, and tomorrow's plan. Then the app stays quiet.

  6. Sunday
    Weekly report.

    Your reflections, speed trends and the links that went up, written to a Markdown file you keep.

Six principles, each taken from a product that earned it

One obvious next step

Today shows a single composed session with one start button. Planning is the app's job.

Things 3 Today view. Raycast's rule: fast, simple, delightful.

Chrome recedes, the work comes forward

The rail and bars sit a few steps dimmer than the canvas and the card. Lines are soft and few.

Linear 2026 refresh: "Don't compete for attention you haven't earned."

The app teaches its own shortcuts

The bottom action bar always lists what you can do here and its key, so you go keyboard-only within a week.

Raycast's action bar redesign.

Understanding clicks through doing

No reading-only lessons. Every concept is a lab, a drill or a prediction you can be wrong about.

Brilliant: "where understanding clicks through doing, not reading."

Accountability without fear

Weekly rings and a 5 of 7 day target instead of a fragile streak. A missed day can be repaired, and there are no guilt notifications at night.

Mobbin's study of 859 streak designs. Lally (UCL): one missed day does not materially affect habit formation.

Your knowledge is a network

Progress uses the words you are learning: links come up, flap when recall drops, and stay down until you build them.

Drawn from the subject itself.

Visual system

Following Linear's approach, the whole palette comes from three inputs: a base neutral, one accent and a contrast level. The colours are defined in OKLCH, so equal lightness looks equal to the eye. Try the controls and the prototype above updates.

Rollover blueThe accent. The colour of the Cisco console cable from your first lab. Focus, selection and progress.
Link upOnly for state: a passed check, a mastered level, a lit port.
FlappingRecall is decaying. Never used for decoration.
DownErrors and wrong predictions, always with the reason next to them.
GraphiteNeutrals carry a faint blue bias toward the accent, never a flat grey.

One family, Geist, for the whole interface. Geist Mono appears only where a real device would print text: configs, addresses, console output and keycaps.

Shape

6 px for keycaps, 9 to 10 px for controls and nodes, 12 to 14 px for panels, 16 px for the window. Nothing else.

Motion

Feedback within 120 ms. A packet hop takes 500 ms on one easing curve. The only orchestrated moment is links lighting in sequence when a lab passes.

Sound, off by default

Three sounds only: a soft two-note rise when a link comes up, a tick per packet hop, and one chord when you close the day.

Density

Calm on Today and Recap, where you decide. Dense in Lab and Console, where you work.

Where it runs

I recommend a Tauri desktop app. The prototype above is built with the same web technology Tauri renders, so this is the look you would actually get, while the simulator runs in native Rust underneath.

Alongside it, a small terminal companion called dojo for quick drills between other work. It shares the same progress file and the same visual language:

Design toolkit now in the repo

These skills are installed in .claude/skills/ so every screen I build follows the same rules. I read each one and checked it for unsafe content first.

frontend-design

Anthropic's official skill. Sets the design brief and the anti-template rules.

taste-skill

The most widely used anti-slop rulebook, with density and motion dials and a long list of AI tells to avoid.

ui-ux-pro-max

A searchable database of styles, palettes, type pairings and UX rules, with a local Python search tool.

redesign-skill

An audit-first process for improving an existing design without breaking it.

baseline-ui

A fast polish pass for spacing, hierarchy and typography.

improve-ui

Structured UI critique with a written plan.

fixing-accessibility

Keyboard, focus, contrast and screen-reader checks.

fixing-motion-performance

Keeps animation smooth and cheap.

output-skill

Keeps generated code complete, with no placeholders.
